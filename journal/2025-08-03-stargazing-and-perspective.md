Drove two hours outside the city into the dark sky reserve. Parked on a gravel turnout surrounded by ancient ocotillo silhouettes. We laid two sleeping bags across the warm metal hood of the Subaru and looked straight up.

Within forty minutes, we saw nine shooting stars—bright green-white streaks slicing across the Milky Way's glowing dust clouds. 

Lying there feeling the night desert wind cool against our faces, all the anxieties about quarterly goals, career trajectories, and inbox backlogs shrank down to atom-sized irrelevance. The universe has been spinning quietly for fourteen billion years before my worries existed, and it will keep spinning long after they are forgotten. 

It wasn't depressing; it was extraordinarily liberating. Everything is light, fleeting, and precious.
