Day two without cellular signal. The first 12 hours felt like phantom itchiness—I caught myself patting my pockets every twenty minutes. But by today, the mental static has dissolved into something wide, still, and deep.

Went on a four-mile trail along the creek. The air smelled intensely of damp cedar bark, moss, and chimney smoke from the wood stove. In the afternoon, chopped kindling and listened to the sharp snap of dry cedar split along the grain. 

Sat by the cast-iron fireplace until midnight reading old essays. My thoughts haven't felt this coherent or unhurried in years. When the mind isn't constantly fielding notifications, it actually finishes its own thoughts.

*Core takeaway:* The noise of modern life isn't inevitable; it's optional. Build offline sanctuaries into every quarter of the year.
