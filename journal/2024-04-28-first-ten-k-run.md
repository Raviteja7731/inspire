At kilometer 6, my quadriceps felt like wet cement. A voice in my head kept offering reasonable-sounding bargains: *"You can stop and walk now. You proved you could do half. Nobody is judging you."*

Instead of listening to the bargainer, I looked down at my shoes, matched my breathing to a 3-count stride, and told myself: *"Just make it to the next lamppost. Then the next."*

When I rounded the final bend along the river and saw the bridge arching overhead with the timing clock, a surge of adrenaline came from nowhere. I sprinted the last 300 meters, chest burning, lungs wide open. Finished in 51:42. 

The medal is just a stamped piece of zinc, but the proof that my mind could gently overrule physical fatigue is permanent.

*Memory anchor:* You are always capable of 20% more than your tired brain claims you are.
