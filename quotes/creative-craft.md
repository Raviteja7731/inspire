### Rick Rubin on the Source of Art
> "The work is an offering to the mystery. The outcome is not in our hands. If we do our best to make something true to ourselves, that is enough."
— **Rick Rubin**, *The Creative Act: A Way of Being*  
*Saved: 2023-09-30*  
*Note to self: Stop evaluating work by how people react in the first 24 hours. Does it ring true to you?*

### Ira Glass on the Creative Taste Gap
> "Nobody tells this to people who are beginners, I wish someone told me. All of us who do creative work, we get into it because we have good taste. But there is this gap. For the first couple years what you make just isn't that good. It's trying to be good, it has potential, but it's not. But your taste, the thing that got you into the game, is still killer. And your taste is why your work disappoints you. A lot of people never get past this phase... Put yourself on a deadline so that every week you will finish one story. It is only by going through a volume of work that you will close that gap, and your work will be as good as your ambitions."
— **Ira Glass**, *Interview on Storytelling*  
*Saved: 2024-03-14*  
*Note to self: Don't give up just because draft one is clumsy. Finish it anyway.*

### Ursula K. Le Guin on Routine and Inspiration
> "A writer is a person who writes. There are no other rules. You don't have to be published. You don't have to make money. You just have to do the work."
— **Ursula K. Le Guin**, *Steering the Craft*  
*Saved: 2024-08-04*  
*Note to self: Strip away the labels. Keep the ritual alive.*

---

### Antoine de Saint-Exupéry on Simplicity
> "Perfection is achieved, not when there is nothing more to add, but when there is nothing left to take away."
— **Antoine de Saint-Exupéry**, *Wind, Sand and Stars*  
*Saved: 2025-04-18*  
*Note to self: When a design or paragraph feels clunky, subtract before you elaborate.*
